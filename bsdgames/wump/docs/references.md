## Historical & Cultural Sources

1. **Yob, Gregory. (1975).** *"Hunt the Wumpus"*. *Creative Computing*, Vol. 1, No. 6 (Sept/Oct 1975), pp. 51–54.
   - *Details:* Gregory Yob's first-person account of inventing the game at People's Computer Company to escape Cartesian grid games.
2. **Ahl, David H. (1978).** *BASIC Computer Games: Microcomputer Edition*. Workman Publishing, New York. ISBN 0-89480-052-3.
   - *Details:* Contains the canonical BASIC listing of *Wumpus 1* and *Wumpus 2*.
3. **People's Computer Company.** (1973). *PCC Newspaper*, Vol. 2, No. 1.
   - *Details:* First public mention and distribution of *Hunt the Wumpus*.
4. **CSRG Archives.** *4.3BSD-Reno and 4.4BSD-Lite source trees*. University of California, Berkeley.

## Technical & Academic Sources

1. **Russell, Stuart J., & Norvig, Peter. (2020).** *Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach* (4th ed.). Pearson.
   - *Section 7.2 (pp. 210–218):* "The Wumpus World" — formal specification of the knowledge-based agent exploring an environment with breezes, stenches, and pits using propositional logic.
2. **Kernighan, Brian W., & Ritchie, Dennis M. (1988).** *The C Programming Language* (2nd ed.). Prentice Hall.
   - *Reference:* Static storage duration, modular arithmetic (`%`), and `strtok()` tokenization safety.
3. **Knuth, Donald E. (1997).** *The Art of Computer Programming, Volume 2: Seminumerical Algorithms* (3rd ed.). Addison-Wesley.
   - *Section 4.5.2:* Euclid's Greatest Common Divisor algorithm ($\gcd$) and modular permutation generators.

## Author Biographies

- **Gregory Yob (1945–2005):** Computer programmer, author, and early pioneer of interactive educational software at the People's Computer Company.
- **Dave Taylor:** Creator of the `elm` email client, early Unix contributor, and founder of Intuitive Systems. Contributed `wump.c` to Berkeley Unix in 1989.
  - Homepage: <https://www.intuitive.com/taylor/>
